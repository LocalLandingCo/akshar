---
title: On Owning Your Words
date: 2026-01-14
tags: [ownership, writing]
excerpt: A platform can change its terms, its algorithm, or its existence. A folder of Markdown files cannot.
---

Every piece you publish on a platform is a small negotiation you didn't get to read the terms of.
The platform hosts the words, but it also hosts the URL, the layout around them, and the decision
about who sees them next.

A file on your own disk has none of that. It is legible to any editor, on any machine, for as long
as text files remain a thing computers understand — which is longer than any single company has
existed.

This is the whole argument for writing in plain Markdown, in a folder you control, backed by a
version history you didn't have to ask anyone for. Everything else is a convenience layered on top
of that fact, and the convenience should never cost you the fact.
