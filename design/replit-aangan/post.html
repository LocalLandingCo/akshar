The courtyard remembers

Every house I grew up in had an aangan — a square of open sky in the middle of things. This is about what it held, and what I had to learn to hold without one.

By The editor·2 Sep 2026·8 min read

In my grandmother's house the courtyard was where the day was sorted. Grain dried there in the mornings, clothes in the afternoons, and by evening it belonged to whoever had a story that could not wait until dinner. Nobody called it a room, but more of our life happened there than in any room.

I did not notice it was a kind of architecture until I lived without one. The flat in the city had corridors instead — spaces for passing through, not for gathering. The weather stayed outside. So, it turned out, did a certain kind of conversation.

अंगणात पडलेली सावली
सांगते दिवसाची गोष्ट.

My grandmother used to say that the shade in the courtyard told you the story of the day. It was not a proverb, just something she said. I have written it down more times than I can count, trying to find the English for it, and each time the sentence comes out a little smaller than hers.

Maybe that is the real subject here: not the courtyard, but the open square in the middle of a language, where things are left to dry in the sun and nobody is in a hurry to put them away.
