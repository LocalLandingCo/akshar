# Writing on your site

A short guide to publishing, in plain language. If something here doesn't
match what you see on screen, ask whoever set your site up — every site
looks a little different depending on what they chose for you.

## Publishing a post

1. Open your editor (the link your helper gave you — either
   `app.pagescms.org`, or `yoursite.com/admin`). Log in with your GitHub
   account.
2. Click **New post**, and choose which section it belongs in (if your
   site has more than one).
3. Fill in a **title**. Everything else — date, tags, an excerpt, a cover
   image — is optional; leave it blank and a sensible default is used.
4. Write. Save as a draft any time — a draft is only ever visible to you,
   never to a reader, until you publish it.
5. Click **Publish**.

**It won't appear instantly.** Publishing rebuilds your whole site, which
takes a few minutes. This is normal — a working site, not a broken one.
Refresh in a few minutes and your post will be there.

## Editing something you already published

Open it in the editor, make your change, publish again. There's no
separate "edit mode" — editing and publishing are the same action, and it
takes the same few minutes to appear.

## Fixing a typo

Same as editing. Open the post, fix it, publish. No one else needs to be
involved.

## Adding a cover image

When you're editing a post, there's an image field near the top. Upload a
photo from your computer — it's resized and optimized automatically, so
don't worry about the file being too big. If you set a cover image, you'll
also be asked for a short description of what's in it (for readers using a
screen reader, and so it doesn't show as a broken image if it ever fails
to load).

## Tags

A free-text list — type a word, press enter, add another. There's no
fixed list to choose from; use whatever's natural. Readers can click a tag
to see everything else you've written with it.

## Drafts

Checking "Draft" keeps a post private — it won't appear anywhere on your
public site, in search, or in your feed, no matter how long it sits there.
Uncheck it and publish whenever you're ready, and only then does it go
live.

## Deleting a post

Open it in the editor and delete it there. Every version of everything
you've ever published is kept in your site's history regardless (that's
what backs your site up — see below), so deleting from the live site is
always safe and always reversible if you change your mind later; ask
whoever set your site up to help you recover an old version if you need
one.

## What doesn't come across from your old platform

If your site started from an import (WordPress, Blogger, Substack), a few
things intentionally don't come with it: comments, subscriber lists, and
view counts. These either need infrastructure this project doesn't have,
or are things platforms hold about _readers_, not your writing — they were
never really yours to bring along. Everything you actually wrote did come
across.

## Backups

You don't need to think about this — it's automatic. Every single change
you've ever published is kept, forever, and can't be lost by publishing
again. If you ever want a copy of everything for yourself, ask your helper
for a full download of your site's content — it's a folder of plain text
files you can open on any computer, no special program required.

## Something's wrong

If publishing doesn't work, or a page looks broken, it's almost always
one of two things: a title was left empty (it's the only field that's
truly required), or a cover image was added without its description. The
editor should tell you which. If it doesn't, or something else seems
wrong, contact whoever set your site up — this is not something you need
to solve alone.
